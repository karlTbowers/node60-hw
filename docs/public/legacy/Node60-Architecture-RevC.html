01

Purpose & design basis

A buildable product architecture defining power domains, signal paths, switching channels, telemetry, software responsibilities, and the preliminary production BOM.

Design requirements

  • Six independently commanded battery-side outputs, each targeting 10 A continuous.
  • Per-output current and voltage monitoring reported over a multidrop 10BASE-T1S network.
  • Current measurement resolves 100 mA steps over the full 0–10 A range; absolute accuracy is specified separately.
  • AEC-Q100 Grade 1 target (−40 °C to +125 °C ambient) or better for active ICs.
  • Four-wire node connection only: T1S twisted pair, battery-direct power, and ground. The controller ECU is solely responsible for bus wake.
  • Low parked-vehicle battery draw, RMII host interface, PLCA follower operation, and Zephyr RTOS.
  • Hardware safety must not depend on the network stack.

02

System architecture

Battery protection, low-power control, logic, network, and six load channels are kept as explicit domains.

Partitioning rule

Keep dirty load current outside the logic return path. The protected battery node feeds both the smart switches and the logic converters. Each output returns directly to the power-entry ground/star region; ADC and PHY grounds join through the quiet logic plane. The T1S coupling and ESD network sit at the connector boundary.

REV C POWER-GATE DECISION

FS26 is removed. A nanopower LDO supplies only LAN8670 VDDAU while parked; LAN8670 INH directly enables the two LMQ66430-Q1 bucks. EN-pin chaining establishes the 3.3 V / 1.5 V order without an SBC, wake latch, or extra logic.

03

Power entry & regulated rails

A protected 60 A-capable VBAT path and deliberately split low-power rails support load control and network wake.

Protected battery path

LM74910HQRGERQ1 controls the DGATE ideal-diode MOSFET bank and HGATE disconnect MOSFET bank for reverse-battery blocking, overvoltage disconnect, programmable overcurrent circuit breaking, and fast short-circuit protection. A shared 0.25 mΩ RSENSE sits between the two FET stages and carries the complete rail current. At 60 A, device count, RDS(on), sharing, copper/bus structure, resistor heating, and fault energy must be calculated together. A 24 V bidirectional XLD8A24CA TVS is preferred after the source fuse; SMBJ24CA is not accepted as the sole primary load-dump element.

MEASUREMENT SEMANTICS

The divider is connected to SW and reports VBAT_RAW on the battery side, before the DGATE ideal-diode FET—not VBAT_PROT. BATT_MON is not sampled while parked; qualify or gate its ADC tap so it does not compromise the ≈50 µA parked budget, then restore it on wake. Use the LM74910H’s separate UVLO pin to define cold-crank behavior and document both networks at schematic review.

Shared protection and total-current measurement

Fit one 0.25 mΩ, 1%, AEC-Q200 RSENSE between the DGATE ideal-diode FET and the HGATE disconnect FET. Both the LM74910H current-sense amplifier and INA228A-Q1 Kelvin-connect with independent star taps at the resistor pads; the high-impedance measurement paths do not interact. Route every downstream branch—including buck, MCU, PHY, and switches—from VOUT after Q2 so RSENSE captures true total node current.

WHY INA228A-Q1 IS PLAN OF RECORD

The shared resistor sees regulator, MCU, PHY, controller, and load current that the six channel readings cannot fully reconstruct. INA228A-Q1 adds only about $0.90 at 1,000 pieces versus an INA240-class analog implementation, avoids an MCU ADC channel, and preserves useful low-current accuracy despite the 0.25 mΩ resistor. Current diverted ahead of RSENSE—including through the primary TVS—remains outside this measurement.

KEEP BOTH VOLTAGE MEASUREMENTS

INA228A-Q1 digitizes the protected rail while the LM74910H SW divider and MCU ADC measure VBAT_RAW before the ideal-diode FET. They observe different nodes and support different diagnostics, so neither replaces the other. The INA228 also reports current and power after firmware programs and verifies SHUNT_CAL for 0.25 mΩ.

DYNAMIC-RANGE & SOURCING

A single 60 A RSENSE cannot also resolve parked sleep current. Parked-drain verification remains a validation-lab measurement. INA240-Q1 and INA229-Q1 were both zero-stock with restocks indicated around February 2027. INA228AQDGSRQ1 is the in-stock plan of record; INA281A2QDBVRQ1 remains the documented lower-cost fallback with substantially higher offset and lower low-current accuracy.

WHY LM74910H-Q1 REPLACES LM74800-Q1

One resistor now serves both protection and precise measurement, cutting shunt heat in half at 60 A. More importantly, the prior LM74800 architecture had no input-rail overcurrent protection; LM74910H adds a programmable breaker, fast short-circuit trip, separate UVLO, FLT warning, and SLEEP-mode OCP auto-retry.

REJECTED ALTERNATIVE — LM74900-Q1

LM74900-Q1 saves about $1 at 1,000 pieces but latches off after SLEEP-mode overcurrent instead of auto-retrying. That is the wrong trade for a battery-parked node whose MCU may be asleep when the fault occurs.

Current-monitor alternatives not carried forward

  • INA186-Q1: 40 V common-mode ceiling leaves no margin for clamped load-dump transients; no authorized stock observed.
  • ST TSC2011: automotive grade, exact part number, and pricing were not verified.
  • Microchip PAC1932/34: no AEC-Q100 qualification.

Power domains

VDDAU_AON

A nanopower LDO supplies only LAN8670 VDDAU while parked. Target LDO quiescent current is about 1–2 µA; exact automotive part remains a schematic-selection item.

3V3_MAIN

S32K344 I/O/analog, LAN8670 VDDA/VDDP, switch VDD, SPI NOR, and clock. The first LMQ66430 is enabled directly by PHY INH.

1V5_CORE

S32K344 VDD_LV via the second LMQ66430-Q1. EN-pin chaining establishes the order and allows at least 250 mA transient margin.

VBAT_PROT

VOUT after Q2 feeds the always-on LDO, both bucks, and all three TPS2HCS08A-Q1 devices so shared RSENSE sees the complete downstream current.

POWER-SEQUENCING HOLD POINT

On bus wake, LAN8670 drives INH high and the external supplies must ramp within 1 ms. Verify EN-chain delay, regulator soft-start, MCU reset release, PHY-ready IRQ_N, and whole-board inrush together. VDDA must never exceed VDDAU by more than 0.5 V; the always-on-first topology satisfies that rule by construction.

FS26 removed from Rev C

The first version stays discrete. FS26’s original roles are now covered by the two LMQ66430 bucks, the S32K344 internal windowed SWT, the TPS3702 rail supervisor, and the LAN8670 INH gatekeeper. Do not place FS26 in the Rev C schematic or BOM. Reconsider an SBC only in a later revision if wake or sequencing requirements outgrow direct INH gating and EN-pin chaining.

Watchdog & power-rail supervision

Use the MCU’s hardened internal window watchdog for software failure detection, and add an independent tight-window supervisor for the 3.3 V MCU/PHY rail.

POR watchdog · S32K344 SWT

Use the internal Software Watchdog Timer. It is window-capable, enabled by default out of reset, and can be hard-locked so runaway code cannot disable or reconfigure it.

Zephyr: nxp,s32-swt · upstream driver

POR rail supervisor · TPS3702-Q1

Add one automotive window supervisor on 3V3_MAIN. Its independent overvoltage and undervoltage detection drives RESET_b.

±0.9% threshold · −40…+125 °C · SOT-6

Watchdog decision

The S32K344 SWT is the plan-of-record watchdog; no external watchdog is fitted. Configure a legal service window, verify reset action, hard-lock its configuration after startup checks, and test early, late, missing, and malformed service sequences. The S32K3 Clock Monitoring Unit already converts monitored clock failure into reset, closing the other major gap an ordinary external watchdog would address. The upstream Zephyr nxp,s32-swt driver avoids custom driver work.

NO EXTERNAL WATCHDOG IN REV C

An external watchdog duplicates the internal windowed SWT without improving rail-fault coverage because it dies with the same supply. If a later revision must support an ASIL safety case, evaluate TPS3430WQDRCRQ1: windowed, AEC-Q100 Grade 1, and observed in stock. It is a future safety-case option, not part of the current BOM. Do not use a TPS3823-Q1-class supervisor/watchdog combination: its non-windowed watchdog is a functional downgrade from the internal SWT.

3.3 V independent window supervision

Add 1 × TPS3702-Q1 to supervise the 3.3 V MCU/PHY rail. The device provides separate overvoltage and undervoltage detection with ±0.9% threshold accuracy over −40…+125 °C and is AEC-Q100 Grade 1 in SOT-6. Its reset output drives RESET_b. Budget about $1; confirm the exact fixed-threshold 3.3 V orderable code, pricing, and authorized stock at BOM lock.

WHY A SUPERVISOR, NOT ANOTHER WATCHDOG

Cold-crank droops and load-dump disturbances can move 3V3_MAIN outside the MCU/PHY operating window. A tight, MCU-independent OV/UV detector catches those conditions before software becomes unreliable; no watchdog can detect a rail fault after its own supply has collapsed. Do not add a separate 1.5 V supervisor: the S32K344 internal low-voltage detector already monitors the core domain.

Identity and PLCA

Firmware stores a unique locally administered MAC address and commissioned PLCA node ID in protected flash. Node ID must be unique and is not hard-coded identically across production units. Coordinator mode is disabled by default.

TC10 bus-wake and complete shutdown

The parked node is not an MCU standby system. It is powered down except for the LAN8670 always-on domain, so the missing S32K3 Zephyr standby implementation is no longer on the critical path. The controller ECU—powered from battery and switched ignition—owns the only wake source and sends the T1S wake-up pattern over the same pair used for data.

FOUR-WIRE SYSTEM DEPENDENCY

The T1S pair is the only wake path. The node cannot wake itself and has no KL15 input; if the controller ECU does not emit the wake pattern, the node stays off. This is a controller-interface requirement, not a recoverable local condition.

WAKE INRUSH & EXACT-TARGET VALIDATION

INH powers the complete switched tree at once. Size the LM74910H TMR breaker ride-through for regulator, bulk-capacitance, MCU, PHY, and output-stage startup inrush. Validate TC10 sleep/wake, INH timing, cold boot, lost-register PHY re-initialization, IRQ_N ready behavior, PLCA restoration, and application readiness on the exact S32K344EHT1MMMST target.

THE 13 A NUMBER NEEDS CONTEXT

The 13 A value is available only with one channel enabled. The all-channels-on line is 8 A per channel at 85 °C. If direct thermal proof fails, derate the output, improve thermal spreading, or move the higher-current revision forward.

SPI control, diagnosis, and reset behavior

Use a simple daisy chain: shared SCLK and CSN, each SDO feeding the next SDI, and the final SDO returning to the MCU. Enable CRC, read back every protection register, and retain one FLT/WAKE line per device. All channels remain off after reset; LHI is hardware-held inactive.

I²t AND LOW-CURRENT MODE INTERACT

KSNS2 improves low-current ratio accuracy, but TI recommends using KSNS2 and 8× ADC scaling only with I²t disabled; altered scaling can lower the effective I²t trip threshold. Rev C keeps KSNS1 while I²t is active and budgets ±18% at 100 mA.

I²t protection profile

  • Program nominal current, I²t trip, fixed-delay overcurrent, immediate shutdown, cooldown, and retry from a harness-specific profile.
  • Enable I²t only after register readback and verified current-sense operation.
  • Keep immediate shutdown active for hard shorts; I²t handles sustained overload energy.
  • Unlimited automatic retry is prohibited. Retain source and branch fusing wherever the switch cannot protect upstream conductors.
  • Log accumulation, trip source, retries, raw current, supply, and temperature.

Conduction and thermal budget

At 10 A, 8.9 mΩ produces 0.89 W per channel at 25 °C; 17.8 mΩ hot typical produces 1.78 W. Six outputs therefore dissipate approximately 5.34 W cold or 10.68 W hot, before connector, copper, regulator, and switching losses.

  • Apply per-sensor alert thresholds, debouncing, plausibility checks, and derating-policy hooks for the NFET bank, shared RSENSE, and three switch packages.

  • Linearize each NTC with a component-specific lookup table or Steinhart–Hart coefficients; do not reuse generic coefficients at production release.
  • Use TMP175 ALERT as an optional independent interrupt path while retaining periodic I²C polling and communication-fault detection.

Fusing

Place the primary fuse at the battery/source end. If the 60 A path is split, fuse each feed and prevent back-powering through an undersized path. Final values coordinate wire ampacity, contact derating, load inrush, PCB fault energy, and the smart-switch curve. Use branch fuses wherever the main fuse cannot protect an output conductor or connector fault.

CONNECTOR FREEZE CONDITION

Connector families are architectural selections, not released mating sets. Freeze exact housings, terminals, seals, wire sizes, and keys together; validate temperature rise with all intended adjacent circuits loaded.

Operating states

RUN

All rails, MCU, full PHY, flash, oscillator, and diagnostics active. Outputs remain off until firmware validates configuration and receives commands.

ENTRY: T1S wake → cold boot → PHY re-init

PARKED / TC10 SLEEP

Only LM74910H SLEEP, the nanopower LDO, and LAN8670 VDDAU remain powered. No logging occurs while parked.

WAKE: controller ECU over T1S only
DEFINED SHUTDOWN TRANSACTION

The controller sends an application-level “go to sleep” message. The node then turns off all six outputs, flushes pending records, waits for page-program completion, commands LAN8670 sleep, and allows INH to collapse the rails. Bus-idle timeout is not used.

Safety, EMC & thermal architecture

This is not a functional-safety certification claim; deterministic safe states and diagnostic coverage are foundations for later hazard analysis.

  • SPI and LHI reset states leave every output off until the MCU verifies configuration and communication integrity.
  • Parked state leaves only LAN8670 VDDAU alive; there is no local ignition or hardwire wake path. The controller ECU must issue the T1S wake pattern.
  • Controller-commanded shutdown is transactional: outputs off, diagnostic-log flush complete, then PHY sleep and INH release.
  • LM74910H-Q1 provides an independent 75 A circuit breaker, default 80 A fast short-circuit trip, FLT pre-trip warning, and SLEEP-mode OCP auto-retry on the 60 A input rail.
  • Hard-lock and window the S32K344 SWT; use the Clock Monitoring Unit for clock-failure reset and TPS3702-Q1 for independent 3V3_MAIN overvoltage/undervoltage supervision through RESET_b.
  • Separate high di/dt loops from SNS/ADC return, oscillator, RMII, and T1S coupling.
  • Start with 2 oz copper and dense thermal vias at every exposed pad; calculate every 10 A path.
  • Budget 5.34 W cold or 10.68 W hot for six full-load channels before other losses.
  • Run ISO 7637-2 / ISO 16750 supply-transient tests and appropriate CISPR 25 conducted/radiated emissions.
  • Validate inductive turn-off energy and every I²t profile against switch, load, harness, and connector.

Net-level rules

  • Name all six outputs and all three SNS nets; document every SPI diagnostic-mux mapping.
  • Route LAN8670 INH directly to the first LMQ66430 EN and chain regulator EN pins for rail order. Do not add FS26, a wake latch, or an MCU-controlled gate in this path.

Verification & release gates

The baseline is ready for schematic capture, not production release. These tests convert architectural assumptions into evidence.

Bring-up sequence

  1. Power-entry coupon: reverse battery, OV disconnect, TVS clamp, cold crank, hot plug, parked leakage, the recalculated 0.6 V OV/UV thresholds, and BATT_MON wake-state accuracy without compromising the parked-current budget.
  2. Input protection: prove the 75 A OCP breaker threshold, default 80 A fast SCP trip, FLT pre-trip signaling, TMR ride-through versus inrush, and SLEEP-mode auto-retry behavior.
  3. Shared-RSENSE path: verify separate star Kelvin routing, INA228A-Q1 I²C communication, SHUNT_CAL for 0.25 mΩ, RSENSE temperature at 60 A continuous, 0–60 A accuracy across temperature, and total-vs-sum cross-check. Confirm buck/MCU rails originate at VOUT after Q2.
  4. Temperature network: verify shared-bus TMP175 communication and optional ALERT, inspect all four NTC placements, spot-check conversion accuracy against a calibrated reference, and test derating/logging thresholds including the TPS2HCS08 I²t telemetry blackout.
  5. Reset supervision: inject 3V3_MAIN undervoltage and overvoltage events, scope TPS3702-to-RESET_b timing, spot-check thresholds across temperature, and fault-inject SWT and CMU reset paths.
  6. Bus-wake power cycle: measure parked current, T1S wake detection, INH assertion, sub-1 ms rail ramp, EN-chain order, whole-board inrush, cold boot, IRQ_N ready, PHY re-initialization, PLCA restore, and application readiness within the accepted ≈1 s budget.
  7. Commanded shutdown: inject the controller’s application-level go-to-sleep message; prove all outputs turn off, pending pages finish programming, PHY sleep is commanded only afterward, INH releases after tSLPINHDLY, and no bus-idle timeout can trigger shutdown.
  8. Flash endurance and recovery: worst-case ten-page pre-sleep flush, ahead-erase, FCB wrap, erase suspend/resume, CRC recovery after interrupted power, log readback over T1S, and NVS counter persistence.
  9. Regulator board: startup, shutdown, load transient, current limit, EN-pin sequencing, and thermal margin across temperature.
  10. MCU-only: package map, clocks, reset, SWD, ADC reference, both LPSPI instances, and Zephyr baseline.
  11. One-switch fixture: daisy-chain framing and CRC, reset-off, SNS mux, FLT/WAKE, output control, and register readback.
  12. Current sweep: all channels 0–10 A; every 100 mA point from 0–1 A; noise, settling, monotonicity, error, supply, and temperature.
  13. I²t fixture: correlate each protection setting against current, temperature, supply, wire, connector, inrush, and short impedance.
  14. Dual-channel thermal: both channels at 10 A at worst ambient in the sealed enclosure—release-blocking qualification.
  15. Six-channel assembly: 60 A matrix, fuse coordination, harness faults, ground offsets, switching during network traffic, EMC, and enclosure thermal.

DECISION SUMMARY

Three TPS2HCS08A-Q1 devices provide six cost-optimized outputs. LM74910H-Q1 and INA228A-Q1 Kelvin-share one 0.25 mΩ RSENSE for total-current telemetry and input protection. Rev C uses a discrete power tree: a nanopower LDO keeps only LAN8670 VDDAU alive at roughly 50 µA typical / 65 µA maximum parked draw, and PHY INH directly enables the LMQ66430 bucks. The controller ECU wakes the node over the T1S pair and explicitly commands shutdown; the node turns all outputs off, flushes its IS25LP032D-JNLA3 diagnostic log, then commands PHY sleep and powers down. FS26 is not fitted. TMP175-Q1 plus four NTCs provide hotspot coverage; the S32K344 SWT remains the watchdog, and TPS3702-Q1 independently supervises 3V3_MAIN. The 10 A target remains release-blocked by the 8 A-per-channel both-on rating at 85 °C.
