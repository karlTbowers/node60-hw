DT13-2P / DTF13-2P added 2026-10-22 (late find, via the Alibaba clone listing below). TE's DT13 family is the right-angle sibling of the DT15 this survey started from (DT13 = 90° pins, DT15 = straight). Both parts are Active and stocked at both distributors — the DT13-2P is the exact connector class originally specced (2-pin, RA, sealed, one per output) at roughly half the ATP13 route's device cost. DTF13-2P is the flangeless variant; without a flange there is no flange seal, so the enclosure-interface seal becomes our detail again (same trade as the MX150 header). 13-week factory lead beyond shelf stock at both distributors.

MX150 sealing caveat. The header is unshrouded pins by design; sealing comes from the plug's mat seal compressed against the enclosure face — standard MX150 device practice, but seal quality becomes an enclosure-design item. Tin contacts, 25 mating cycles. Plug housing 33472-0402 is obsolete; the current part is 33472-4001 (29k in stock). Used one-per-output, the two blades per pole are paralleled (~5 A/pin at 10 A out) and current capacity is a non-issue.

The current capability is genuinely comfortable: 18 A/contact, and the specs' temperature-rise curves (fully loaded pairs) give only ≈8 °C rise at 10 A on 14 AWG. It is out on paper, not on copper:

  1. The temperature ceiling is +120 °C, not +125. Section 4.3 of both product specs rates the system −40…+120 °C operating. Molex's part pages and most distributor parametrics say +125 °C (DigiKey's 19418-0017 listing matches the spec at +120). The product spec governs: at a 125 °C ambient the connector is past its ceiling before any current flows, and the allowable ambient for 10 A continuous works out to ≈112 °C on 14 AWG. Contrast the DT size-16 contact, whose governing spec (108-151009) rates 13 A at 125 °C.
  2. Molex explicitly disclaims this application. Section 8 of both specs states the system is IPX7-rated and conforms to UL 1977 but is NOT suitable for automotive applications with requirements such as USCAR-2 (also named: USCAR-25, GMW3191, and others); the MX150L family page repeats the disclaimer. This is not a missing qualification — it is an explicit exclusion of the application class.
  3. Secondary: sealing per the spec is IPX7 (the product pages say IP67), one grade below the DT's IP68/IP6K9K; and no flanged RA PCB header exists — flanged panel-mount parts in this family are on the 19429 plug side only, so the enclosure would owe the wall seal on all six outputs.

Kept on record so the question does not get re-asked. The same +120 °C ceiling and USCAR disclaimer apply to MX150L's entry on the T1S survey's PCB-mount shortlist (signal-level current does not cure an over-ceiling ambient); its status there is flagged in that document and is Karl's call. The v1 DT13-2P decision is unaffected.

Geometry & enclosure obligations

  • The TE solder pins are straight. Keying discipline. The cavity we mold implements Key A only. The user documentation must say “Key A / black housing” — Key B (33472-4002, light gray), Key C (-4003, brown) and Key D (-4004, green) plugs will not seat in a Key A cavity. User tooling: any open-barrel crimper; Molex's own hand tools are 63811-6000 (18–22 AWG) and 63811-5900 (14–16 AWG); terminal extraction tool 63813-1500.

What our molding must provide — the cavity duties

The 75900 header contributes only the blades. Everything the plug expects from a production blade housing, our enclosure molding has to replicate:

  1. Ring-seal bore — the plug's integral ring seal seals radially against the inside wall of the shroud. The bore needs the correct diameter, depth, and a smooth sealing surface; this bore is the environmental seal for the whole output.
  2. Key A polarization ribs — so only a Key A plug seats, and it cannot be inserted rotated.
  3. Latch catch — the geometry the plug's integral locking lance snaps onto; without it the plug has no retention.
  4. Blade entry alignment — 2×2 at 3.50 mm pitch, registered to the soldered strip; the 12.15 mm blade length sets the cavity depth budget.

Design references (Molex public documents): AS-75900-208 (application specification for this header family — the device-integration guide), PS-75757-000-001 (product specification, incl. current derating), SD-75900-001-001 (header sales drawing); plug side: PS-33472-000-001 and AS-33472-100-001. Wiring patterns

Same choice as Route A's MX150:

  • Paralleled pair per pole (recommended): both blades in a row carry one pole — ≈5 A per blade at 10 A out, inside every published rating including the product-spec derating curves; users wire 18–20 AWG (33012-2002).
  • Single blade per pole: one 4-circuit header carries one output on two blades, or two outputs one-per-blade-pair across two headers; the governing check is the PS-75757-000 derating table at 125 °C ambient with all circuits loaded — headline 17 A is not the number to design to at temperature. Users wire 14–16 AWG (33012-2001).

Variant cost recap

  • Device side: $6.48/device in pin strips (6 × $1.08) + the enclosure molding — vs $20.04/device in Deutsch pins for the DT variant, with no pin-retention or bending work.
  • User side: ≈$2.40 per plug, all parts stock distributor items — vs ≈$1.30 for the DT plug, which is also sold as ready-made kits by motorsport suppliers; the MX150 plug is a build-it-from-parts item.

Industrial, unsealed (for completeness): Phoenix Contact AKG terminals clamp onto a busbar (AKG 4 BU: 41 A, DIN rail); WAGO and Wieland have nothing in this class; TE CROWN CLIP Jr (170 A) and AMP PDS Busplug (500 A) are board-to-busbar clips — a different problem.

The OEM stamped-busbar pattern — terminals integral to a stamped copper plate inside the box — is how production PDUs do this, but those systems (Yazaki/Sumitomo class) are OEM-program-only with no distributor presence. Doing it here means custom stamped metalwork in the enclosure: adjacent to Route C, not a catalog buy. Assessment: for a 10 A output, the only sealed + busbar + stocked candidate is the SurLok Plus 3.6 mm busbar-rear — 5–7× oversized per pole, one housing per output. The HVP800 is the only part at the right current and it fails on sealing evidence (no published IP) and channel (960-pc OEM minimum). Recorded for later revisions; the v1 DT13-2P decision is unaffected.

Decision — DECIDED 2026-10-23

DECIDED 2026-10-23 (Karl): the genuine TE DT13-2P is the output connector for the first revision of this device. Six per device, one per output, genuine DT06-2S as the user plug (≈$42/device mated at ~100-pc breaks; 508 in stock at Mouser / 217 at DigiKey on 2026-10-22).

Derating check (run 2026-10-23) — CLOSED: PASS, with conditions. TE defines the Deutsch size-16 contact rating as a continuous rating at 125 °C: 13 A (TE common-contact performance specification; DT product spec 108-151009 §3.2 gives the same 13 A with 14 or 16 AWG wire, 10 A with 18 AWG, 7.5 A with 20 AWG; connector range −55…+125 °C). At 10 A per output the DT13-2P runs at 77% of its rating at the maximum rated ambient — there is no further ambient derating to apply, because the rating is already stated at 125 °C. Contact drop is spec-limited to 100 mV at 13 A (≤ ~77 mV / 0.77 W per contact at 10 A, worst case at the spec limit). Conditions: (1) user wiring 16 AWG minimum — 18 AWG is rated exactly 10 A with zero margin; the user documentation should spec 14 AWG (green-band sockets 0462-209-16141) for margin; (2) the 125 °C figure is the air temperature at the connector — an enclosure interior hotter than that puts every other part in the design out of spec as well; (3) PCB copper at the header must be sized for 10 A per output (layout item). System note: the connector is not the binding constraint on simultaneous 10 A outputs — the TPS2HCS08 thermal gate (8 A/ch with both channels at 85 °C) binds first. Side finding from 108-151009: TE lists header ingress protection as “Not Tested” (IP68/IP6K9K is the inline rating) — the DT13-2P’s device-side seal is its flange seal; recorded for the enclosure design.

Everything else in this survey stays on record for later revisions: Route B (panel mount) and Route C (integrated socket, DT or MX150 geometry) as surveyed alternatives, and the DT13-2P clones as a cost-down candidate (~$20–33/device saving) gated on the sample-validation work described in the clone subsection. A bus-bar-termination pass was added 2026-10-23 (section above): no sealed ≤30 A/pole busbar connector exists in distribution — recorded for later revisions, v1 decision unaffected. A screw-clamp/threaded-stud pass was added the same day (section above): the screw-rear pattern exists at output class (Buccaneer, C016, Han E), threaded-stud rears are input-class only, and the DT class offers neither — recorded for later revisions, v1 decision unaffected. Molex MX150L was also checked for output duty on 2026-10-23 (Route A, above) and set aside on Molex's governing specs — system ceiling +120 °C and an explicit §8 statement that the system is not suitable for USCAR-2 automotive applications — at ≈$44/device, so cost was never the issue; v1 decision unaffected.
