10BASE-T1S Connector Survey

Part numbers, pricing, and stock for the node60's single 10BASE-T1S port — one connector carrying the single twisted pair, either a 2-pin housing or a 4-pin housing with the pair on two cavities and the unused cavities sealed. Karl's starting idea: the same kind of connector as the outputs (Deutsch DT family), at signal scale. The survey's first-class criterion is mis-mating protection: the T1S plug must not be able to mate with the 10 A output receptacles (DT13-2P headers taking DT06-2S plugs), and vice versa. Six routes surveyed: DT 2-way, DTM, Superseal 1.5, Aptiv GT 150 / Metri-Pack 150, Molex MX150, and Ethernet-native NanoMQS / MATEnet. No T1S connector is selected yet; the companion output survey is decided (genuine TE DT13-2P) and the input survey is undecided. Correction applied 2026-10-23: the device side must be a true PCB-mount header — right-angle or straight vertical, through-hole or SMD — that solders to the board like the DT13-2P outputs, not an inline cable housing. The live candidates are collected in the PCB-mount section — 2-pin and 4-pin headers only. Restructure 2026-10-24: connectors that exist only as inline cable housings have been moved to their own record-only section; they stay documented, but they are not candidates. Second correction, 2026-10-23: the MX150L headers in that shortlist were listed at −40…+125 °C from Molex part pages and distributor parametrics; Molex's governing product spec (PS-19427-001-001) rates the system −40…+120 °C and disclaims USCAR-2 automotive use — MX150L is recorded in the PCB-mount section as checked and set aside, a flagged concern awaiting Karl's call.

UNDECIDED — restructured 2026-10-24: live section is PCB-mount only (2-pin & 4-pin); DT13-4P / AT13-4P-BM03 front-runners · MX150L flagged · inline-only routes moved to record-only section Prices & stock checked 2026-10-23 · 4-pin pass 2026-10-24 DigiKey / Mouser

Requirement. One connector carrying the 10BASE-T1S pair (single twisted pair, 10 Mbps multidrop) — a 2-pin housing, or a 4-pin housing with the pair on two cavities and the rest sealed. Signal current only — milliamps; contact current rating is a non-issue for every candidate here. The PHY also receives its TC10 wake-up over this pair while the node sleeps, so the port is a plain pass-through: no magnetics, no termination inside the connector (bus termination lives at the two ends of the bus, not at this drop). Under-hood: −40…+125 °C, sealed (IP67 class or better), same environment as the rest of the device.

Mis-mating protection is a first-class criterion. A data cable on a power output — or a load cable on the PHY pair — is the failure this survey is designed to rule out mechanically, not by labels.

PCB-mount device side (added 2026-10-23). The port must mount on the PCB exactly like the outputs do: a right-angle or straight-vertical header, through-hole or SMD, presenting the sealed interface at the enclosure wall. Inline cable housings on the device side do not meet this requirement, however good their other numbers are — see the PCB-mount section. 4-pin housings qualify (2026-10-24): the pair runs on two cavities, the unused cavities are sealed with cavity plugs, and a 4-pin plug cannot mate the 2-pin output headers — mis-mating protection is preserved. Connectors available only as inline housings are grouped in the record-only section and are not candidates.

Basis. All prices USD, checked 2026-10-23 at DigiKey and Mouser unless noted. “Per device” = 1 port: device housing + user housing + 2 contacts, @1.

DT13-4P — identical to the outputs in everything except the plug it accepts. Same RA flange header, same IP68/IP6K9K and −55…+125 °C ratings, same tooling and flange wall seal — the flange seals the enclosure wall itself, exactly as on the outputs. Complete port ≈ $14 @1 (header + DT06-4S + contacts + two cavity plugs). The cost of the trick: a 4-cavity housing for a 2-wire port, and two sealed dummy cavities.

MX150L — checked and set aside (2026-10-23); a flagged concern, and Karl's call whether it stays listed or is struck. On the numbers it was a genuine contender: the only true 2-position sealed PCB header family, cheaper than either TE option (RA header $5.77, plugs from $3.84), and electrically comfortable — Molex's temperature-rise data shows only ≈8 °C rise at 10 A on 14 AWG against an 18 A-per-contact rating (≈112 °C allowable ambient for 10 A continuous). Two findings set it aside. Temperature ceiling: Molex part pages and distributor parametrics say −40…+125 °C, but the governing product specification PS-19427-001-001 (§4.3) rates the system −40…+120 °C — at a mounting location whose air temperature can reach this device's +125 °C requirement, the system is over its ceiling regardless of how little current the T1S pair carries. Pedigree: §8 of the same spec states MX150L is not suitable for automotive applications with requirements such as USCAR-2; it is an IPX7 / UL 1977 industrial system (governing-spec sealing: IPX7). The lesser debits stand as originally recorded: no flanged right-angle PCB header exists in the line — the flanged variant (19417-0042) is a cable-side receptacle, not a PCB header — so the enclosure would owe the wall seal; and this is the MX150L family, distinct from Route 5's inline MX150, which has no 2-way header. Recorded as a checked-and-set-aside entry, following the MCP 9.5 pattern from the input survey. The full output-duty check behind these numbers is recorded in the output survey's MX150L entry, where the line is likewise checked and set aside — v1 outputs stay DT13-2P.

AT13-4P-BM03 — the strongest 4-pin fit, and the DT13-4P's direct rival. Same right-angle through-hole flange mount, IP67 and −55…+125 °C on distributor data, header $8.76 at DigiKey (212 stock; Mouser 8 at $7.74 with a tariff note) against the DT13-4P's $12.70 there, plug AT06-4S $1.54 with ≈13k stock — complete port ≈ $11.85, and full kits run ≈$10. The DT and AT 4-way systems intermate, so the two headers are functionally interchangeable at 4 positions; neither plug can mate a 2-pin output. Cavity sealing is documented for both: Amphenol's A114017 size-16 cavity plugs are specified for DT and AT housings alike.

Why 4-pin works for a 2-wire port. The T1S pair occupies two cavities; the other two are sealed with cavity plugs (114017 / A114017, $0.35 / $0.11, stock in the hundreds of thousands). The pair's geometry and the PHY's view of the link are unchanged. What the extra cavities buy is selection: the 4-pin class is where sealed PCB-mount flange headers actually exist — in the DT/AT ecosystem the 4P header is a catalog part with deep stock, while no sealed 2-pin flange header exists at all. The NanoMQS 4-pos row shows the failure mode to avoid: a 4-pin header with no cavity plug available for the unused positions leaves two open cavities in a sealed port.

776540-1 correction. The “Superseal PCB header” that stood as this shortlist's third member in the previous revision does not exist: 776540-1 is an AMPSEAL 16 inline housing (see its struck row above and Route 3). With it struck, there is no third survivor — the live field is the DT13-4P / AT13-4P-BM03 pair, the MX150 4-circuit header, NanoMQS 4-pos with its cavity-plug gap, and the flagged MX150L headers.

Verified negatives — checked for a PCB header, none qualifies

  • Amphenol AT13-2P-BM — intermating with DT CONFIRMED, out. Amphenol's own drawing names the AT06-2S (= DT06 interface) as its mate and DigiKey cross-references AT13 against DT13 directly: it is a drop-in DT13-2P equivalent ($5.41–5.93, in stock), so it fails mis-mating in exactly the same way as straight DT.
  • TE AMPSEAL / AMPSEAL 16 — no 2-pin header, and no standard 4-pos header either. Smallest standard AMPSEAL PCB header is 8 positions, and the plain tin versions are rated only −40…+105 °C anyway. The 4-position parametric hits that once suggested an AMPSEAL 4-pos header resolve to AMPSEAL 16 inline housings — the same trap that misfiled 776540-1 (see the struck row above).
  • No 4-pin PCB headers in the sensor families. Superseal 1.5 has no 4-pos PCB header (inline only — indeed no PCB header in any position count); Aptiv offers no 4-way PCB header; JST JWPF is sealed and 4-pin but its header caps at +85 °C; Hirose DF62W has no 4-pos and caps at +105 °C.
  • TE NanoMQS — no 2-pos PCB header, and no sealed header at all. Sealed NanoMQS seals at the ECU housing around an unsealed internal header; the sealed 2141467-1 in Route 6 is cable-side only.
  • TE MATEnet — header fails temperature and sealing. The standard header is unsealed (Sealable: No) and rated −40…+105 °C; the sealed HD MATEnet hybrid takes PCB headers but runs $40+ with zero stock.
  • JAE MX23A starts at 12 positions; H-MTD is unsealed; Aptiv offers no 2-way sealed PCB header (smallest is an 8-way special-order part); DTM and MX150 (Routes 2 and 5) are inline-only at 2-way, as originally reported.

Inline-only connectors — record only, not candidates

Routes 1–6 below are the original survey's families. Each was moved here on 2026-10-24 for one reason: on the device side they exist only as inline cable housings — none offers a PCB-mount header (right-angle or vertical, through-hole or SMD) that meets the requirement, so none can be selected for this port, however good its other numbers are. They stay in the document so the work, pricing, and verified negatives aren't lost or re-litigated. Two cross-references point the other way: the DT family's 4-pin header (DT13-4P, Route 1's family one size up) is a live candidate in the PCB-mount section, and Route 3 records the 776540-1 correction — the part once misread as a Superseal PCB header is an inline housing from this section's world, not the live one.

ROUTE 6

Ethernet-native — NanoMQS / MATEnet

TE NanoMQS — sealed signal system, not an Ethernet connector

TE NanoMQS is a miniature signal system (0.5 mm blades) that TE does not designate as an Ethernet/SPE connector — no impedance-controlled 2-pin UTP variant exists. A sealed 2-position housing does exist: 2141467-1 (IPX9K per Mouser, −40…+130 °C housing rating; terminals rated 170 °C). On the device side, NanoMQS has no 2-pos PCB header and no sealed header at all — sealed NanoMQS seals at the ECU housing around an unsealed internal header. Its 4-pos header (2315405-1, RA SMD) does exist and is carried in the PCB-mount section — with the caveats that the header is unsealed at part level and no cavity plug for the unused positions was found.

Assessment

Surveyor's assessment, for the decision.

  • Under the PCB-mount requirement, the live field is a 4-pin contest: DT13-4P vs AT13-4P-BM03. Both are right-angle through-hole flange headers in the intermating DT/AT 4-way system; both are mis-mate-safe by cavity count; both seal their unused cavities with documented size-16 plugs (114017 / A114017). DT13-4P is the literal “just like the output connectors” answer — same header, wall seal included, ≈$14–15/port complete. AT13-4P-BM03 matches it on mount and temperature (−55…+125 °C) at IP67 and ≈$11.85/port complete — the strongest 4-pin fit on price. Behind them: the MX150 4-circuit header (≈$4/port, but the header carries no IP rating and the enclosure owes the face seal) and NanoMQS 4-pos (real RA SMD header, −40…+130 °C, but unsealed at part level and no cavity plug exists for its unused positions).
  • MX150L is a flagged concern, in both sizes. Its 2-pin headers (RA $5.77 / vertical $4.84) are joined by 4-pin headers (19427-0032 $7.54 / 19428-0011 $7.05) — and the 4-pin parts inherit the flag exactly: the governing spec (PS-19427-001-001) rates the system −40…+120 °C, not the +125 °C its part pages advertise, and §8 disclaims USCAR-2 automotive use. Cheaper and genuinely PCB-mount, but over the temperature ceiling on paper; whether it stays listed as a flagged concern (the MCP 9.5 pattern) or is struck is Karl's call. It also has no flanged RA PCB header, so the enclosure would owe the wall seal.
  • The “Superseal PCB header” is struck — it never existed. 776540-1 is an AMPSEAL 16 inline housing; Superseal has no PCB header in any position count. The earlier third-place shortlist slot is gone, and the correction is recorded in both the live section and Route 3 so it isn't re-derived.
  • The inline-only routes (record-only section) are documented, not candidates. DTM (Route 2) remains the balanced pick of that set — Karl's “same kind as the outputs” instinct satisfied safely, ≈$3.61/port complete — and Superseal 1.5 (Route 3) its value pick at ≈$2.14/port; both are disqualified for this port solely by the absence of a PCB-mount device side.
  • Superseal 1.5 (Route 3) is the value pick for the inline set — ≈$2.14/port complete with enormous stock — trading down to IP67 and a lighter-duty contact system that a signal pair will never stress; its 776540-1 header carries the family into the PCB-mount shortlist.
  • MX150 (Route 5) is the keying pick: the only full A–D keying system at 2-way, from a family already in this project's surveys — held back by an unpublished IP code and a temperature split that both need one product-spec check.
  • DT (Route 1) is disqualified on a verified fact, not taste: 2-way DT cannot be keyed, so it cannot be made mis-mate-proof against the outputs.
  • Aptiv (Route 4) works but brings an unpublished IP code and a Mouser-only channel; Ethernet-native (Route 6) brings pedigree without a clean under-hood fit.
