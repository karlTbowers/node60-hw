**Version 10 is chosen** (<Ref id="DEC-023" />, 2026-11-01): version 7 (<Ref id="DEC-022" />) on a 2.0 mm board. A raised band across the front over the outputs, a separate hood over the T1S pair, staggered receptacles in the low middle of the lid, and a removable north panel; the busbars rest on the REDCUBE spigots. The PCB holds it. The other designs, kept as candidates from 2026-10-30, stay here as the record of the options; version 9 (2026-11-01) is version 7 made as small as it goes. Each has its own page with a dimensioned drawing, a dimension table and a 3D mockup. All of them are generated from the same connector placement script, so every version stays consistent with the parts and with the checks below.

## How the versions differ

- **Version 1** (<Ref id="DEC-020" />, superseded): the board hangs component side down under a flat lid. The SurLok studs reach the SMT REDCUBEs through the board, so the joint is metal to metal and the whole stud thread engages. With the headers hanging below, it is the tallest version. Its plugs may point west, north or east, but the cable check fails: a plug pointing inward, or north from the front pair, runs its cable over another receptacle. It is kept as the record of DEC-020, not reworked.
- **Version 2** (<Ref id="DEC-021" />, superseded): the board sits component side up, with busbars on top of it and the SMT REDCUBEs underneath. A raised T covers the outputs and the T1S pair on the north wall, and the receptacles sit in the low sections either side of it. The T1S pair is 40 mm apart (45 until 2026-10-31), so the stem is narrow enough for the receptacles beside it to stagger: battery 57.5 mm from the centre line and in front (50 mm from the front edge), return 78.7 mm, so a battery plug's cable pointing north passes beside the return receptacle. Version 4 is the same.
  - **Building the case (2026-10-31):** the board has headers on the north and south walls, so it can't go into one-piece walls. The north wall is made as a separate panel: the board is lowered 15.1 mm north of its place, slid south so the outputs go through the one-piece front wall, and then the north panel slides 13.4 mm south onto the T1S shrouds and is bolted to the floor and side walls with a gasket; the lid goes on last. Every header still seals on a closed cutout. Because the board slides over its standoffs, a 15.1 mm strip on the south side of each mounting hole must stay free of parts on the underside. The front wall, with the six outputs and their harness loads, stays one piece with the base.
- **Version 3**: the T1S pair moves to the side walls next to the outputs, so a single raised band across the front covers every header. The power receptacles sit behind the band, staggered: battery pair close, return pair wide, so a battery plug pointing north passes beside the return receptacle.
- **Versions 4 and 5**: versions 2 and 3 with REDCUBE **press-fit** 786202106 terminals. The block stands 10.5 mm on the board and the busbar sits on top of it, so:
  - **No laminate in the joint:** the busbar bears on the block, not on the board, and the stud goes into a 6 mm blind thread.
  - **Parts can go under the busbars**, if they are lower than about 10 mm.
  - **The lid is nearly flat:** in version 5 the low sections end up only 2.6 mm below the raised ones (4 mm lid). A thicker busbar would lift the receptacles, but costs stud engagement: 3/16" leaves about 2.9 mm of thread, 1/4" about 1.4 mm.
  - **Version 4 has a flat lid** (2026-10-31): flat at the headers' height, with a 31 mm square seat machined for each receptacle, so the receptacles sit where they need to with the 1/8" busbars and the block's whole 6.0 mm thread engaged. The seats are 12.55 mm deep since the SurLok correction of 2026-11-02 (2.6 mm before), and the lid is thickened underneath each seat so it stays 4 mm thick there. Only the press-fit pins (3 mm) hang under the board, so it sits 5 mm off the floor, and the enclosure is 46.1 mm tall, 0.8 mm more than versions 2 and 7 at their raised sections. Like version 2, it has a removable north panel.
  - **Stock:** the 786202106 had no distributor stock when it went into the library (2026-10-26). Each block also needs a 4 mm component keep-out and 3 mm from the board edge.
- **Version 6**: version 2 with the outputs spread over three walls, two on each: OUT1-2 on the west wall, OUT3-4 on the south wall and OUT5-6 on the east wall, numbered going round the board. The T1S pair stays in the middle of the north wall. Each side wall has one output in its back corner and one in its front corner, so a power plug pointing outward passes between them. A raised band across the front covers the south and front-corner headers; the back-corner headers and the T1S pair each get their own raised section. The receptacles are staggered as in version 3, battery pair close and in front, return pair wide, so a battery plug pointing north passes beside the return receptacle. The battery pair sits 52 mm either side of the centre line so its cables, pointing north, pass beside the T1S hood rather than over its edge. The board is 139 mm deep instead of 160 (user, 2026-10-31), about the least that passes every check: from back to front, the back-corner hoods, the return row, the battery row (2 mm between the flanges, and a battery plug pointing north stops short of the T1S hood) and the front band.
- **Version 7** (<Ref id="DEC-022" />, chosen 2026-11-01, then made 2.0 mm thick as version 10): version 2 without the T's stem. The raised band across the front still covers the outputs, but the T1S pair on the north wall gets its own raised hood, so the lid between them is low. The receptacles and busbars are placed as in version 6 (52 and 78 mm from the centre line), on the full 160 mm board: battery pair close and in front, return pair wide. Plug directions are as in version 2: outward or north. The T1S pair is 40 mm apart, as in version 2.
  - **Building the case:** as version 2: a removable north panel, carrying the T1S hood's step.
- **Version 8**: version 7 with a second board for the microcontroller under the main board. The T1S pair moves onto it, in the same positions on the north wall, and stands up through a notch in the main board's north edge. The main board rises to leave 10 mm between the boards, which is what lets the T1S headers fit under the low lid with 3 mm to spare (9.5 mm is the minimum), so the T1S hood goes and only the front band over the outputs stays raised. The cost is height: the whole enclosure is 8 mm taller than version 7. The MCU board's outline is a placeholder, and splitting the schematic into two boards (and the connector between them) isn't done, so this version can't be placed on the PCB yet.
  - **Fitting the boards:** a header's shroud passes through a closed cutout, so a board can only go in by sliding toward its headers' wall; a single board with headers on two walls (every other version) needs a split case. In version 8 each board has headers on one wall only: the MCU board is lowered in 13.4 mm south of its place and slid north into the north wall, then the main board is lowered 15.1 mm north of its place and slid south into the front wall. So the main board is 15.1 mm shorter (250 × 144.9 mm) and ends that far from the north wall, and its notch for the T1S headers is 15.1 mm further south. `scripts/check_assembly.py` checks every version.
  - **Mezzanine (placeholder):** the boards join through a stacking connector. The main board slides in 2.5 mm high and drops onto it, guided by two pins, so the output cutouts are 2.5 mm taller than Amphenol's drawing; that leaves 2.31 mm of panel seal above each cutout instead of 4.81. The connector, its stroke and its position are placeholders until one is chosen. This is a candidate only: the board split is not an architecture decision.
- **Version 9**: version 7 with every connector as close to the next as the checks allow, to find the smallest board for that design: **230 × 110 mm** (version 7: 250 × 160), 37 % less board area. Outside, 237.7 × 117 × 45.25 mm. Plugs pointing west or east may reach past the side walls (2026-11-01); in every other version they must end inside the board edge. It is a candidate only; the PCB stays on version 7.
  - **Headers:** 1 mm between courtyards. The outputs are at a 37.3 mm pitch (AT13 courtyard 36.3 mm), and the T1S pair is 39 mm apart (ATM13 37.9 mm). The panel seals are then 2.1 mm apart on the front wall, and the outer outputs' seals are 5 mm from the side walls.
  - **Width:** the outputs set it. The outer outputs' panel seals must clear the inside corner of the machined base, assumed R5 (a 10 mm cutter for a pocket about 30 mm deep), so the side walls' inside faces are 115.85 mm from the centre line. The board is a round 230 mm and stops 0.85 mm short of them (0.5 mm elsewhere): only the front wall locates it, so the side gap can be anything. Its edge is 3.6 mm past the outer courtyards. While outward plugs had to stay inside the walls, a return plug pointing outward (46.1 mm from its receptacle) set the board at 249.5 mm. The receptacles sit where the plugs pointing north need them: battery 53 mm from the centre line, so its plug passes 3 mm beside the T1S hood, and return 78.5 mm, so its flange is 3 mm beside that plug. A plug pointing outward reaches up to 5.8 mm past the side wall's outside face, and its cable runs on from there; leave that room around the node where it is mounted.
  - **Depth:** a stack from the north edge. The T1S courtyards reach 26.85 mm; the GND busbar is 1 mm south of them (return row at 40.6 mm); the battery row is 29 mm further, so the flanges are 2 mm apart; and the battery flange is 2 mm north of the front band (40.1 mm from the front edge).
  - **Every one is at its limit:** moving any of these 0.5 mm tighter fails the plug check or the corner rule. The return row is the exception: the plug check doesn't cover it, and the GND busbar sets it.
  - **Parts:** the other 258 parts on the schematic need about 2,650 mm² of courtyard. That leaves about 10,600 mm² of the top side outside the connectors and busbars (version 7: about 25,400), plus the bottom side. Routing, the 10 A output copper and the heat from the switches and the input MOSFETs need more than the courtyards. The thermal test (10 A is still unproven) decides whether the smaller board is enough.
- **Version 10** (<Ref id="DEC-023" />, chosen): version 7 on a standard 2.0 mm board instead of 2.6 mm (2026-11-01). Everything else is version 7's. 2.0 mm is as thick as JLCPCB builds 6 and 8 layers; at 2.6 mm only PCBWay quoted, at about 2.5 times the price.
  - **The REDCUBE spigot stands 0.6 mm proud.** The 74660016R's spigot is 2.6 mm long, so the busbar rests on the two spigots, 0.6 mm above the board. The busbar top, the receptacles, the low lid and the stud engagement stay where version 7 has them.
  - **No laminate in the joint:** tightening the receptacle clamps the busbar onto the spigot, and the load stays in the REDCUBE. The board carries none of it, so DEC-021's clamped-laminate concern goes away.
  - **Lower raised sections:** the headers stand on a board 0.6 mm thinner, so the front band and T1S hood drop 0.6 mm: a 20.5 mm step and 44.7 mm overall. Parts under the low lid get 0.6 mm more headroom (9.0 mm above the board).
  - **Header pin tails:** the AT13 and ATM13 tails come through 0.7 to 1.5 mm instead of 0.1 to 0.5 mm, which settles DEC-020's marginal solder joints. The #4-20 header screws are 0.6 mm shorter.
  - **Current:** unchanged. The board's current capacity comes from its copper, not the core thickness, and 2.0 mm carries the same layer count and copper weights; its vias are 23 % shorter, so heat moves between layers slightly better. Keep other nets' copper out from under the busbars, which no longer rest on the solder mask.
  - **Open:** Würth's pages give no board thickness for the 74660016R. Confirm with Würth that the spigot may stand 0.6 mm proud of a 2.0 mm board.
- **Version 11**: version 9 (version 7, compact) on version 10's 2.0 mm board (2026-11-01), a candidate only. The plan is version 9's: a **230 × 110 mm** board, outputs at a 37.3 mm pitch, the T1S pair 39 mm apart, and plugs pointing west or east allowed to overhang the side walls. The heights are version 10's: the busbar rests on the REDCUBE spigots 0.6 mm above the board, and the front band and T1S hood are 0.6 mm lower (44.7 mm overall). Outside, 237.7 × 117 × 44.7 mm. Version 9's limits and version 10's open point (Würth's OK for the proud spigot) both apply.
- **Version 12**: version 11 on a 1.6 mm board instead of 2.0 mm (2026-11-01), a candidate only. The plan is version 9's (**230 × 110 mm**). The REDCUBE's 2.6 mm spigot stands 1.0 mm proud and the busbar rests on it, so the receptacles, the low lid and the stud engagement stay as in versions 9 to 11. The front band and T1S hood are 1.0 mm lower than version 9's: a 20.1 mm step and 44.25 mm overall; parts under the low lid get 9.4 mm. The header pin tails come through 1.1 to 1.9 mm. Open, beyond version 10's (Würth's OK for a spigot standing 1.0 mm proud): the board is about half as stiff as a 2.0 mm one (stiffness goes with the cube of the thickness), under the headers' and busbars' weight in vibration.
- **Version 13**: version 11 with Amphenol **RADLOK** 5.7 mm receptacles instead of SurLok Plus (2026-11-01), a candidate only: feed-throughs RL9057-101-F1RE (battery, red) and RL9057-101-F1 (return, black), with right-angle plugs RL00571-25RE and -25BK for 25 mm² cable, all 120 A, 1000 V, −40 to +125 °C; IP67 as first listed here, but Amphenol Tuchel lists RADLOK as IP40 mated ([Power receptacles](./receptacles/)). All four were in stock at Mouser on 2026-11-01 (647 to 812); DigiKey had none of the RL00571-25BK. The plan is version 11's, except the battery receptacles sit 53.5 mm from the centre line instead of 53.
  - **Higher on the busbar:** the receptacle's panel face is 14.2 mm above its contact face (drawing: 24.2 to the stud tip less the 10 mm stud; Amphenol's STEP gives 13.35, and the drawing is used, user's decision 2026-11-02), against SurLok's 9.25 (drawing C10-730187; 19.2 before the correction of 2026-11-02), so the low lid is 6 mm higher than in the SurLok versions and parts under it get 15.0 mm. The stud seats the receptacle on the busbar and the flange screws seat it on the lid, so the lid has to be at this height within the seal's give.
  - **A 3 mm panel at most:** the keys in the Ø14.7 hole are that long. The 4 mm lid has a 29 mm square seat 1 mm deep machined into its top at each receptacle, so the receptacle mounts on 3 mm. The M4 screws still get the Ø8 × 4 mm bosses (7 mm of thread). The keys stop the housing turning in the lid, so the stud has to be tightened (4.0 N·m on the drawing) some other way; the pin has a hex at the bottom of the receptacle's tube, which suggests it turns inside the housing. Confirm how Amphenol means it to be fitted.
  - **Stud:** 10 mm (drawing), against SurLok's 9.95. Both take the REDCUBE's whole 5.6 mm thread and come out below it, so the board sits 6.2 mm off the floor (6.17 with SurLok), keeping the stud tip 2 mm above the floor.
  - **Heights:** the low lid is lower (30.2 mm outside, with 15.0 mm under it above the board, against version 11's 19.0; the tallest part on the board is the 5.6 mm debug header), but the band and hood are 1.2 mm higher because the board is: 44.7 mm overall, a 14.5 mm step.
  - **Plugs:** the RADLOK plug is shorter (37.25 mm ahead of the axis against 46.1) and 0.5 mm wider (18.5). The wider plug moved the battery receptacles 0.5 mm out to keep a north plug 3 mm from the T1S hood. All 16 directions clear; the tightest is 3.2 mm (a battery plug pointing north to the return flange).

<Aside type="caution" title="SurLok geometry corrected (2026-11-02)">
Until 2026-11-02 the SurLok versions used the library model's estimated 7.7 mm stud and took 19.2 mm as the receptacle's seat-to-panel depth. Amphenol's SLPRATPSB drawing C10-730187 and STEP give a 9.95 mm stud beyond the seat and 9.25 mm from the seat to the panel face (19.2 is the panel face to the stud tip). With the corrected figures, in the busbar versions (2, 7 and 9 to 12):

- **The low lid drops 8.8 mm** (outside 24.2 mm high), and parts under it get about 9 mm above the board instead of 19. The tallest parts under the low lid haven't been checked against this yet.
- **The lid's M4 bosses end 1.25 mm above the busbar.**
- **The stud takes the REDCUBE's whole 5.6 mm thread** (4.5 mm before) and comes out below it, so the board sits 6.17 mm off the floor instead of 5; the raised sections rise with it by 1.17 mm (44.7 mm overall on a 2.0 mm board).
- **The plug, cable and assembly checks are unchanged:** they are in plan.

See [Power receptacles: RADLOK vs SurLok Plus](./receptacles/) for the comparison. The decision records DEC-020 to DEC-022 keep the figures they were made with.
</Aside>

## Sources and assumptions (all versions)

- **Positions:** `scripts/place_connectors.py` (`VERSIONS`) holds every version's layout. Each board edge sits 0.5 mm behind the connector flange face it meets, so the flange, not the board, meets the wall.
- **Geometry:** `scripts/enclosure_geometry.py` builds each version's enclosure: the board height, the terminal and busbar stack, and the raised sections, derived from the header bodies plus a 3 mm clearance. `scripts/enclosure_versions.py` draws them all with the handbook's enclosure-drawing engine.
- **Plug check:** `scripts/check_surlok_clearance.py` tries every allowed plug direction against the other plugs, the flanges and the raised sections, keeps each flange 2 mm from a raised section and from the other flanges, and runs each plug's cable straight on past the walls, 2 mm clear of every raised section in plan. The cable is taken as 11 mm across (assumed, typical for 25 mm²). A plug pointing west or east must stay inside the walls, except in version 9, where it may overhang them.
- **AT13 cutout:** Amphenol drawing C-AT13-2P-BMXX rev. A2, "recommended panel cutout"; the mating axis is 13.8 mm from the board.
- **ATM13 cutout:** Amphenol drawing ATM13-2P-BMXX rev. A2. The 15.0 mm axis is measured from Amphenol's STEP model; the drawing gives no height.
- **SurLok Plus:** Amphenol customer drawing C10-730187 rev. F and customer STEP (SLPRATPSB product page): the contact face (the back of the stud's hex shoulder) is 9.25 mm below the panel face and the stud is 9.95 mm long beyond it; catalog IC-10, p. 13 for the panel layout ([Power receptacles](./receptacles/)).
- **RADLOK (version 13):** Amphenol's customer drawings "RADLOK Feed Thru Pin To Thread" rev. K and "Tubular Lug Version" rev. L, and the STEPs measured for the DropOut library's models (`enclosure_geometry.RECEPTACLES`).
- **Terminals:** Würth 74660016R (SMT, 2.6 mm spigot, 5.6 mm through thread; flush on a 2.6 mm board, 0.6 mm proud of version 10's 2.0 mm one) and 786202106 (press-fit, 10.5 mm block, 6.0 mm blind thread).
- **Assumed:** 3 mm walls and floor, machined from solid aluminium. A 4 mm lid (2026-10-31), with an Ø8 × 4 mm boss under each SurLok receptacle's four M4 holes, so the threads get 8 mm of material; the bosses stay 1.6 mm clear of the Ø14.3 receptacle hole. The low lid's outer face is set by the receptacles, so the thicker lid costs 1 mm of headroom inside (9.0 mm above a 2.0 mm board in versions 10 and 11) and raises the raised sections' tops by 1 mm. The bosses end 1.25 mm above the busbar top. Amphenol gives no panel-thickness limit for the receptacle (catalog IC-10, assembly instructions AI-9): its tail passes through the Ø14.3 hole and it seals under its flange. With the board up: 8 mm from the floor to the board; 5 mm in version 4 (2026-10-31), and 6.17 mm in versions 2, 7 and 9 to 12 and 6.2 mm in version 13, set by the stud passing through the REDCUBE with its tip 2 mm above the floor, where only 3 mm hangs under the board (the SMT REDCUBE bodies or the press-fit pins). With it down: 3 mm under the deepest header. 3 mm between a header and a raised section; 1 mm above the headers in versions 2, 7 and 9 to 13 (2026-10-31), which makes their step over the SurLok low lid about 21 mm (45.25 mm tall on a 2.6 mm board, 44.7 mm on 2.0 mm). Busbars are 1/8" × 1" C110 copper flat bar, cut and drilled in house.
