The enclosure isn't chosen yet, so eight mechanical designs are kept as candidates (user, 2026-10-30). Each has its own page with a dimensioned drawing, a dimension table and a 3D mockup. All of them are generated from the same connector placement script, so every version stays consistent with the parts and with the checks below. The PCB holds one version at a time: **version 2** (<Ref id="DEC-021" />) until a version is chosen.

## How the versions differ

- **Version 1** (<Ref id="DEC-020" />, superseded): the board hangs component side down under a flat lid. The SurLok studs reach the SMT REDCUBEs through the board, so the joint is metal to metal and the whole stud thread engages. With the headers hanging below, it is the tallest version. Its plugs may point west, north or east, but the cable check fails: a plug pointing inward, or north from the front pair, runs its cable over another receptacle. It is kept as the record of DEC-020, not reworked.
- **Version 2** (<Ref id="DEC-021" />): the board sits component side up, with busbars on top of it and the SMT REDCUBEs underneath. A raised T covers the outputs and the T1S pair on the north wall, and the receptacles sit in the low sections either side of it. The T1S pair is 40 mm apart (45 until 2026-10-31), so the stem is narrow enough for the receptacles beside it to stagger: battery 57.5 mm from the centre line and in front (50 mm from the front edge), return 78.7 mm, so a battery plug's cable pointing north passes beside the return receptacle. Version 4 is the same.
  - **Building the case (2026-10-31):** the board has headers on the north and south walls, so it can't go into one-piece walls. The north wall is made as a separate panel: the board is lowered 15.1 mm north of its place, slid south so the outputs go through the one-piece front wall, and then the north panel slides 13.4 mm south onto the T1S shrouds and is bolted to the floor and side walls with a gasket; the lid goes on last. Every header still seals on a closed cutout. Because the board slides over its standoffs, a 15.1 mm strip on the south side of each mounting hole must stay free of parts on the underside. The front wall, with the six outputs and their harness loads, stays one piece with the base.
- **Version 3**: the T1S pair moves to the side walls next to the outputs, so a single raised band across the front covers every header. The power receptacles sit behind the band, staggered: battery pair close, return pair wide, so a battery plug pointing north passes beside the return receptacle.
- **Versions 4 and 5**: versions 2 and 3 with REDCUBE **press-fit** 786202106 terminals. The block stands 10.5 mm on the board and the busbar sits on top of it, so:
  - **No laminate in the joint:** the busbar bears on the block, not on the board, and the stud goes into a 6 mm blind thread.
  - **Parts can go under the busbars**, if they are lower than about 10 mm.
  - **The lid is nearly flat:** in version 5 the low sections end up only 2.6 mm below the raised ones (4 mm lid). A thicker busbar would lift the receptacles, but costs stud engagement: 3/16" leaves about 2.9 mm of thread, 1/4" about 1.4 mm.
  - **Version 4 has a flat lid** (2026-10-31): flat at the headers' height, with a 31 mm square seat 2.6 mm deep machined for each receptacle, so the receptacles sit where they need to with the 1/8" busbars and 4.5 mm of stud in the thread. The lid is thickened 2.6 mm underneath each seat, so it stays 4 mm thick there. Only the press-fit pins (3 mm) hang under the board, so it sits 5 mm off the floor, and the enclosure is 46.1 mm tall, 2 mm more than versions 2 and 7 at their raised sections. Like version 2, it has a removable north panel.
  - **Stock:** the 786202106 had no distributor stock when it went into the library (2026-10-26). Each block also needs a 4 mm component keep-out and 3 mm from the board edge.
- **Version 6**: version 2 with the outputs spread over three walls, two on each: OUT1-2 on the west wall, OUT3-4 on the south wall and OUT5-6 on the east wall, numbered going round the board. The T1S pair stays in the middle of the north wall. Each side wall has one output in its back corner and one in its front corner, so a power plug pointing outward passes between them. A raised band across the front covers the south and front-corner headers; the back-corner headers and the T1S pair each get their own raised section. The receptacles are staggered as in version 3, battery pair close and in front, return pair wide, so a battery plug pointing north passes beside the return receptacle. The battery pair sits 52 mm either side of the centre line so its cables, pointing north, pass beside the T1S hood rather than over its edge. The board is 139 mm deep instead of 160 (user, 2026-10-31), about the least that passes every check: from back to front, the back-corner hoods, the return row, the battery row (2 mm between the flanges, and a battery plug pointing north stops short of the T1S hood) and the front band.
- **Version 7**: version 2 without the T's stem. The raised band across the front still covers the outputs, but the T1S pair on the north wall gets its own raised hood, so the lid between them is low. The receptacles and busbars are placed as in version 6 (52 and 78 mm from the centre line), on the full 160 mm board: battery pair close and in front, return pair wide. Plug directions are as in version 2: outward or north. The T1S pair is 40 mm apart, as in version 2.
  - **Building the case:** as version 2: a removable north panel, carrying the T1S hood's step.
- **Version 8**: version 7 with a second board for the microcontroller under the main board. The T1S pair moves onto it, in the same positions on the north wall, and stands up through a notch in the main board's north edge. The main board rises to leave 10 mm between the boards, which is what lets the T1S headers fit under the low lid with 3 mm to spare (9.5 mm is the minimum), so the T1S hood goes and only the front band over the outputs stays raised. The cost is height: the whole enclosure is 8 mm taller than version 7. The MCU board's outline is a placeholder, and splitting the schematic into two boards (and the connector between them) isn't done, so this version can't be placed on the PCB yet.
  - **Fitting the boards:** a header's shroud passes through a closed cutout, so a board can only go in by sliding toward its headers' wall; a single board with headers on two walls (every other version) needs a split case. In version 8 each board has headers on one wall only: the MCU board is lowered in 13.4 mm south of its place and slid north into the north wall, then the main board is lowered 15.1 mm north of its place and slid south into the front wall. So the main board is 15.1 mm shorter (250 × 144.9 mm) and ends that far from the north wall, and its notch for the T1S headers is 15.1 mm further south. `scripts/check_assembly.py` checks every version.
  - **Mezzanine (placeholder):** the boards join through a stacking connector. The main board slides in 2.5 mm high and drops onto it, guided by two pins, so the output cutouts are 2.5 mm taller than Amphenol's drawing; that leaves 2.31 mm of panel seal above each cutout instead of 4.81. The connector, its stroke and its position are placeholders until one is chosen. This is a candidate only: the board split is not an architecture decision.

<Aside type="caution" title="SurLok stud length">
Amphenol doesn't publish the M6 stud's length; every engagement figure here uses the library model's estimate of 7.7 mm. In versions 2–8 the 3.175 mm busbar uses up part of the stud before it reaches the terminal's thread. Get the real length before choosing.
</Aside>

## Sources and assumptions (all versions)

- **Positions:** `scripts/place_connectors.py` (`VERSIONS`) holds every version's layout. Each board edge sits 0.5 mm behind the connector flange face it meets, so the flange, not the board, meets the wall.
- **Geometry:** `scripts/enclosure_geometry.py` builds each version's enclosure: the board height, the terminal and busbar stack, and the raised sections, derived from the header bodies plus a 3 mm clearance. `scripts/enclosure_versions.py` draws them all with the handbook's enclosure-drawing engine.
- **Plug check:** `scripts/check_surlok_clearance.py` tries every allowed plug direction against the other plugs, the flanges and the raised sections, keeps each flange 2 mm from a raised section and from the other flanges, and runs each plug's cable straight on past the walls, 2 mm clear of every raised section in plan. The cable is taken as 11 mm across (assumed, typical for 25 mm²). A plug pointing west or east must stay inside the walls.
- **AT13 cutout:** Amphenol drawing C-AT13-2P-BMXX rev. A2, "recommended panel cutout"; the mating axis is 13.8 mm from the board.
- **ATM13 cutout:** Amphenol drawing ATM13-2P-BMXX rev. A2. The 15.0 mm axis is measured from Amphenol's STEP model; the drawing gives no height.
- **SurLok Plus:** catalog IC-10, p. 13 (panel layout) and p. 6: the contact face is 19.2 mm from the panel face.
- **Terminals:** Würth 74660016R (SMT, 2.6 mm spigot, 5.6 mm through thread) and 786202106 (press-fit, 10.5 mm block, 6.0 mm blind thread).
- **Assumed:** 3 mm walls and floor, machined from solid aluminium. A 4 mm lid (2026-10-31), with an Ø8 × 4 mm boss under each SurLok receptacle's four M4 holes, so the threads get 8 mm of material; the bosses stay 1.6 mm clear of the Ø14.3 receptacle hole. The low lid's outer face is set by the receptacles, so the thicker lid costs 1 mm of headroom inside (18.4 mm above the board) and raises the raised sections' tops by 1 mm. Amphenol gives no panel-thickness limit for the receptacle (catalog IC-10, assembly instructions AI-9): its tail passes through the Ø14.3 hole and it seals under its flange. With the board up: 8 mm from the floor to the board; 5 mm in versions 2, 4 and 7 (2026-10-31), where only 3 mm hangs under the board (the SMT REDCUBE bodies or the press-fit pins), leaving 2 mm. With it down: 3 mm under the deepest header. 3 mm between a header and a raised section; 1 mm above the headers in versions 2 and 7 (2026-10-31), which makes their step over the low lid 11.1 mm (44.1 mm tall). Busbars are 1/8" × 1" C110 copper flat bar, cut and drilled in house.
